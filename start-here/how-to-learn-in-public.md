# How to learn in public

Learning in public means showing small parts of what you are learning, even before you feel like an expert. It can be a note, a post, or a project. Sharing is optional; private practice counts too.

## How your progress becomes visible

1. Use a Public repository when you want to showcase your skills. It lets anyone open your notes and projects; a Private copy is for practicing before you are ready to share.
2. Save meaningful changes with **Commit changes** on GitHub. Each commit is a saved change with a date, creating a history of what you added or improved. The [visibility steps](../GETTING-STARTED.md#make-your-progress-visible) explain how to open that history.
3. Share your own repository link. The link is your public proof of the work you have shared: people can read your explanations, try your projects, and inspect the changes. A commit count alone does not prove mastery.
4. Choose a routine you can sustain. Consistency matters more than volume: one useful note or improvement is better than many empty updates. Breaks are fine, and sharing every day is not required.

## Public proof checklist

This is an optional guide for when you want to showcase your learning, not a deadline. You can build it up one small step at a time.

1. [ ] My repository is **Public**.
2. [ ] I have at least one dated daily log in `my-learning/`.
3. [ ] I update `my-learning/progress-tracker.md` weekly while I am studying.
4. [ ] I have one project folder in `my-learning/projects/` with a `README.md` explaining what I made and how to try it.
5. [ ] I have shared my own repository link somewhere, such as a bio or post.
6. [ ] I have added the topic `upskill-in-public` to my repository.

The project folder and tracker are yours to create; they are not prefilled work. The [workspace guide](../my-learning/README.md) explains how to create them.

### Add the repository topic

A topic is a short label that helps people find related repositories. Adding it does not publish a Private repository.

1. Open your own repository's main page on GitHub.
2. Find **About**, then select the gear icon beside it (**Edit repository metadata**).
3. Under **Topics**, type `upskill-in-public` and select the matching topic or press Enter to add it.
4. Select **Save changes**. Check that the topic appears under **About**.

## A simple routine

1. Study one small topic for a time that fits your day.
2. Write what you learned in your own words using a [daily log](../templates/daily-log.md).
3. If you feel ready, share one small thing: a post, a note, or a project link. A sentence about what worked and what confused you is enough.
4. Choose one next step. There is no need to post every day or compare your pace with anyone else's.

## Post ideas

| Idea | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Daily log | Today I made a button respond to a click. |
| A concept explained simply | A variable is a name for a value my program can use. |
| Mini project | My word counter works for a short sentence; next I will try an empty input. |
| Certificate or milestone | I finished my first module and can explain one new idea. |

## Before sharing

1. Share only your own notes and work. Link to resources instead of copying them, and never upload paid course content.
2. Remove passwords, access keys, personal details, and information about other people.
3. Check any school rules before sharing assignment work. Keep the post private if you are not ready.

# Google Cloud Platform roadmap

## Before you start

This original route starts with cloud concepts and Google Cloud Digital Leader, then introduces practical work and a choice of future role. Certification means earning a credential through the provider's assessment. The exam is optional; these study checkpoints are not a complete exam syllabus or a guarantee of passing.

1. Read the [cloud introduction](../intro/README.md). No previous cloud experience is needed. For your first 15 minutes, write one sentence each explaining compute, storage, and networking in your daily log.
2. Choose one free reading resource from [resources](resources.md). Reading and the written project below need no cloud account. Services, labs, and exams may cost money even when the documentation is free.
3. Use short regular sessions and move on when you can show each checkpoint. If you know a topic already, demonstrate its checkpoint first. Save your work in [my-learning](../../../my-learning/README.md).

## Stage 1: Foundations

1. Explain public, private, and hybrid cloud using the introduction. Compare running your own equipment with using a provider's services.
2. Learn infrastructure as a service (renting basic computing resources), platform as a service (a managed place to run apps), and software as a service (a finished application). Give one example use for each.
3. Learn how Google Cloud projects organize services and how billing is connected to them. Compare Compute Engine (virtual computers) and Cloud Storage (object storage for files) for a study app.

Checkpoint: A labeled study-app diagram showing where code runs, where files live, and how people connect. An explanation of each choice matters more than memorizing service names.

## Stage 2: Fundamentals certification study

1. Open the [official Google Cloud Digital Leader guide](https://cloud.google.com/learn/certification/cloud-digital-leader) and compare its current topic list with your notes. Certification reference checked on 2026-10-16; providers may revise their outlines. For Google Cloud, the certification page links to the current exam guide.
2. Explain why an organization might change how it runs applications or uses data. Explore data analysis, artificial intelligence (systems that perform tasks such as recognizing patterns), access controls, and operating costs through simple use cases. Cloud Digital Leader emphasizes concepts and business decisions, not proof of hands-on engineering ability.
3. Write an explanation and a small real-world example for each topic group in the official outline. Use official sample or practice material where freely available, and revisit concepts you cannot explain. Paid exam-preparation products are optional.
4. Decide whether taking the exam fits your goal and budget. Check the official page for current requirements and fees before booking. Continuing to practical learning does not require passing the exam.

Checkpoint: Your own topic checklist, examples, and a list of areas to revisit. Sample-question scores alone do not prove exam readiness.

## Stage 3: Build and check

This stage extends beyond fundamentals exam study. It introduces engineering thinking through a small project, not a complete job training program.

1. Design a simple study-notes service. Label its compute, storage, connections, and who may read or change data. Use fictional data.
2. Write what would happen if access were denied, the service stopped responding, or costs increased. Explain how you would notice and respond. Include a cleanup plan and remember that budget alerts do not guarantee spending stops.
3. Choose a written project or an optional hands-on exercise from official documentation. For hands-on practice, review pricing first, follow the guide's access and billing setup, test normal and denied access, then remove practice resources and check for remaining charges. Keep credentials out of notes and source code.
4. Save your design, expected results, and findings using the [mini-project template](../../../templates/mini-project.md). Label a paper design as a design, not as a deployed service.

Checkpoint: A documented design with access rules and a cost plan, or a small tested deployment with cleanup evidence.

## Stage 4: Share and continue

1. Share your own diagram or notes when ready. Explain the difference between what you designed, what you tested, and what you still need to learn.
2. Choose one role direction from the [cloud introduction](../intro/README.md): operating services, developing applications, designing systems, or working with data.
3. Build practical foundations next: operating-system basics, networking, Git (tracking file changes), and a scripting language (code for automating repeated tasks). Start with one small task such as reading a local log file and explaining the result.

Checkpoint: A chosen role direction and one concrete next practice task.
